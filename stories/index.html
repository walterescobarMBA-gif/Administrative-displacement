Stories and case studies

Use real records, not invented examples.

Case material should show how an administrative sequence worked while protecting participant privacy and preserving source boundaries.

Publication standard

A story is not evidence just because it is compelling.

Administrative Displacement does not publish fictionalized participant scenarios as if they were empirical examples. Published case material should be grounded in identifiable records, attributed participant reports, organization responses, or clearly labeled analysis.

Where a source contains private or sensitive participant information, the public explanation may be de-identified, summarized, or withheld while the underlying evidence is preserved for appropriate verification.

Evidence labels

Every case study should tell readers what kind of information they are seeing.

01Documented fact

Supported by an identified record.

02Attributed report

What a participant or organization says occurred.

03Analysis

An interpretation of the sequence, labeled as analysis.

04Unresolved question

A material point the available record does not answer.

Current use

Documented examples are now built into the framework.

The plain-language definition page includes de-identified examples involving pathway visibility, compressed deadlines, application format, sponsor/subcontractor coordination, and inspection-sequence questions. Each example states both what the preserved record establishes and what it does not establish.
